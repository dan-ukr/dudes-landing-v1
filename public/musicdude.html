MusicDude

Your music taste, mapped.

MusicDude listens to the songs you already love and draws them as a sky: every song a star, placed by how it sounds. Then it uses that map to send you the song for this moment while you listen, and to find your eras, your people and your gigs.

Bring the music you already have

Years of liked songs and playlists are already a diary. MusicDude starts from them, not from a quiz.

  • Your whole Spotify or Apple Music library, through TuneMyMusic, in a couple of taps
  • Streaming history exports, with the date of every play
  • Playlist and album links, ListenBrainz, or songs searched by hand
  • Or just listen: on Android, it builds your portrait from what you play

Why it matters. Nothing to rebuild and nothing to rate. The taste you already have is the starting point.

It listens, song by song

An audio model listens to a 30-second clip of every song in your library.

  • Mood, energy, brightness and the instruments you can hear
  • The language of the vocals, found by separating the voice from the music
  • The artist's country and the song's era, from open music databases

Why it matters. When something can't be measured, it says "unknown". Nothing is guessed, so the map you get is honest.

Your portrait

Your taste as a picture: a constellation of every song, and the outline of the space you live in.

Next to it: your moods, eras, the countries your music comes from on a world map, your languages and genres, and a short story about what it all says about you. Tap any part to open exactly those songs.

Why it matters. Most apps tell you what you played. This shows who you are as a listener, in one image you can share.

Your eras

Your listening history, cut into the stretches where your music changed.

Every era gets a name you can change, its defining artists, its top five songs, a monthly chapter, and its own little sky. Then drag through all of them on one map and watch the dots drift and the outline reshape, year by year.

Why it matters. Music is how people remember where they were. Your eras give those years a shape and a name.

One song for right now

You don't have to open the app. While you listen, MusicDude notices when your music settles into a mood, and sends you one song for that moment.

MusicDude now

Right now: late-night and melancholic

"Teardrop" by Massive Attack fits this moment. Tap to play.

It works from what you're actually playing in Spotify, YouTube Music, Apple Music or any other player. It stays in the language and scene you're in: a run of Ukrainian rap gets Ukrainian rap, not a random hit. It's one song, with a line on why it was chosen, never a playlist of fifty. And it only comes when there's a real moment: silence is the normal state.

The same listening keeps your portrait current by itself, no imports needed.

Why it matters. Choosing is the hard part. The right song, arriving at the moment you need it, is the thing no feed can do.

A song for today

Five songs a day, drawn from what you really played in the last 24 hours.

"More of this" brings a close relative. "Change the vibe" goes somewhere else and keeps that song away. Alongside it, daily picks: some close to your taste, some further out, for discovering.

Why it matters. Every answer teaches it. The more you steer, the closer the picks get.

Your library, sorted by how you feel

Filter by language, mood, era, region, tempo and more. Then sort by the one thing no other app can: how close each song sounds to your current mood, as a percentage.

  • Discover the whole catalogue through the same filters, each song with its match to your taste
  • Ask for music in your own words: "like this, but calmer, from the nineties"
  • Build playlists and send them to Spotify or Apple Music
  • Tell it what never to recommend: a language, a region, a genre
  • Remove one song or a whole group, and the portrait recalibrates at once

Why it matters. A big library is only useful if you can find the right song in it. This makes it searchable by feeling.

Games made of your music

Short games built from your own library: guess the era, the mood, the artist, where a song is from, whether it's even yours.

  • A Daily Drop every day, and a streak with freezes for the days you skip
  • A weekly league: climb from Vinyl to Cassette, CD and Gold Record
  • Specials around the big nights: Eurovision, the Grammys, the Super Bowl

Why it matters. Two minutes a day, and you hear your own music with fresh ears.

Your people

Put your sky next to a friend's and see the match in one number.

What you share, where you split, the songs you'd send each other. Challenge them to swipe the same ten songs and find out who knows whose taste. Then blend both libraries into one playlist.

Why it matters. Friends see your taste, never your moods. You share the music, not the diary.

Gigs that fit your taste

Upcoming concerts near you, sorted by how well each artist matches your sky.

See which friends are going, add a gig to your calendar, play a few songs before you buy, and get an alert when a matching artist announces a show nearby. Alerts keep only a rounded area of about 10 km, never your exact location.

Why it matters. The best night out is the one you would have missed.

It talks your way

Eleven languages, and five ways of talking to you. Pick one.

Why it matters. An app about your taste should sound like it was made for you.
